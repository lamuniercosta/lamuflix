## Q5 — Metric formulas and denominators

Keel question: Define metric formulas and denominators. I recommend HR@10 = count of target observations whose movie appears in the first 10 final-ranked distinct movies divided by all target observations; MRR = mean of 1/rank for that same top-10 list, zero when absent (explicitly MRR truncated at 10, never full-list MRR). Every row counts once including repeated movie plays, cold-start, short/empty recommendation lists and targets excluded by the real ranking policy; no favorable-row filtering or deduplication of target observations. Duplicate movie IDs in ranking output are invalid rather than silently repaired. Empty input yields observationCount=0 and both metrics=0, with the count exposing that no evaluation occurred. This aligns the ticket top-10 scoring with reproducible comparison; cost is miss penalties for unreachable targets and an explicit MRR cutoff. Please rule whether these semantics preserve the ticket, correcting them if needed.

Patron answer: Confirmed: HR@10 = count of target observations whose movie appears in the first 10 final-ranked distinct movies divided by all target observations; MRR = mean of 1/rank over that same top-10 list (0 when absent, explicitly truncated at 10); every playback row counts once (including repeated plays, cold-start, short/empty lists, and policy-excluded targets, with no favorable-row filtering or deduplication); duplicate movie IDs in ranking output are invalid and must fail rather than be silently repaired; and empty input yields observationCount=0 and both metrics=0 (citing DEV-356:18, recon-DEV-356:13-14, DEV-416:34-35,40, and PRODUCT.md §3 lines 23-24).

Decision, rationale, and implications: HR@10 and MRR use all playback observations as denominator, with MRR explicitly truncated at 10. Rank is one-based; absence contributes zero. Repeated plays, cold starts, short or empty lists, and policy-excluded targets remain observations. Duplicate recommendation IDs fail validation. Zero observations produce zero metrics and explicit zero count. These rules avoid favorable filtering and make edge cases deterministic. DEV-416 note citations are Patron-supplied, not independently verified here.
